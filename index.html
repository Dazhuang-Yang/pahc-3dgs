Abstract

While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has revolutionized real-time photorealistic rendering, its unstructured and discrete nature incurs substantial storage and transmission overhead, impeding its deployment on resource-constrained edge platforms. Existing 3DGS compression methods are predominantly driven by signal-level redundancy, leaving object-level structural regularity and material sparsity untapped in industrial assets such as aerospace targets. To explicitly capitalize on these physical priors, we propose a physics-aware hierarchical 3DGS framework that shifts the compression paradigm toward semantic-level structural reassembly. Specifically, a geometry layer condenses repetitive structures into compact meta templates via a coarse-to-fine cascade: spectral divergence pruning, robust rigid alignment, and alternating differentiable refinement. Subsequently, an appearance layer exploits material sparsity through component-wise vector quantization. Extensive evaluations across diverse aerospace scenarios, including high-fidelity synthetic models and real-world mock-ups, demonstrate that our method effectively copes with the rate-distortion bottlenecks of current compression paradigms. It reduces the explicit 3D representation to approximately 2 MB, achieving a storage compression ratio of at least 5.5$\times$ while maintaining high-fidelity visual quality above 30 dB. By synergistically decoupling spatial topology and material statistics, our method preserves structural integrity and delivers a robust, memory-efficient 3D representation for bandwidth-constrained autonomous perception.

Method Overview

The overall pipeline of the proposed framework. First, an unstructured Gaussian field is decomposed into semantic components. Then, a hierarchical representation is constructed with a geometry layer and an appearance layer. Finally, the compact representation is packed for efficient storage and real-time rendering.
